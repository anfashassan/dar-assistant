Haya, a property assistant for DarGlobal and Wasalt

Haya answers questions about DarGlobal's projects and searches Wasalt's property listings, in English and Arabic. Every fact comes from the two companies' public websites, every price from a real listing, and answers link back to their sources.

The problem

Someone looking at a DarGlobal project or a home on Wasalt has questions that are spread over many pages: which projects are in Oman, when a tower will be finished, what a villa in north Riyadh costs, whether there is anything under 2 million riyals in Jeddah. Today that means browsing two websites, project pages, filters and articles. Haya puts both sites behind one conversation.

Who it is for

  • Buyers and investors comparing DarGlobal's branded residences across six countries.
  • Home seekers in Saudi Arabia searching Wasalt by city, budget, bedrooms and sale or rent.
  • Arabic speakers, with full Arabic questions, answers and a right-to-left interface.
  • DarGlobal and Wasalt teams, as a prototype of an assistant that could sit on their own sites.

What it does

Answers with sources

Facts are cited 1 and link to the DarGlobal or Wasalt page they came from. If the sites don't say something, Haya says so instead of guessing.

Listing search

"Villas for sale in Riyadh under 2M" becomes filters over real listings: count, median price, and a spread of options across districts and prices.

Conversations

Follow-ups keep their context: "what about Jeddah?", "only the newest ones", "how many bedrooms do the villas have?" after asking about a project.

English and Arabic

Ask in either language, get the answer in the same one. Arabic questions are matched to English pages.

Figure check

After each answer, every price and year is checked against the sources. The answer shows "6 figures checked against sources", or flags any figure it can't find.

Voice input

Speak a question in English or Arabic. The text appears in the box to edit before sending.

How it works

  1. Collect. A scraper reads darglobal.co.uk (project pages, press, blog, FAQ) and wasalt.sa (listings, city pages, blog). Listings are stored as structured records.
  2. Understand the question. Small talk is answered directly. Otherwise Haya pulls out what it can: city, district, property type, budget, bedrooms, project names, country.
  3. Find the facts. A search over 16,000 text passages finds the relevant pages; listing questions also run through a structured filter, so prices always come from real records.
  4. Answer. A free language model (via OpenRouter) writes the answer from those sources only, with citations. Haya then checks the figures it used.

Testing found real problems, each now fixed and covered by a test:

  • The currency "SAR" was read as the Riyadh district "Al Samer", silently filtering every price question.
  • Asked to compare two projects, Haya found pages for only one and said the other didn't exist.
  • Some listings showed the price per square metre as the full price, so "the cheapest land in Dammam" was SAR 1,828.
  • If the model list couldn't be loaded at startup, the app fell back to a source that sometimes returned empty answers.
  • A project still mentioned in older articles (L'Horizon by Elie Saab) no longer has a page on DarGlobal's site; Haya now says so.

Design decisions

  • Prices only from data. Listing questions go through filters over real records; the model never invents a price.
  • Exact search first, meaning-based search as a backup. Name-based search is precise for projects and districts. A small multilingual model adds matches for reworded questions ("where is it located?" vs "headquartered in London"). Mixing the two into one ranking made name questions worse (first-result accuracy dropped from 0.89 to 0.72), so the meaning-based results get the last two of six slots instead.
  • Built for a free model. One model call per message, answers cached for an hour, free models discovered automatically with fallbacks. If no model answers, Haya still shows the matching sources and listings.
  • Runs anywhere Docker runs. One container with the data and the search model inside; it starts in about 8 seconds and re-reads Wasalt every 24 hours.

Limits

  • Listing prices are as of the scrape date (November 2026); Wasalt is refreshed daily, DarGlobal's pages are not.
  • Free models take 3 to 15 seconds per answer and have daily limits.
  • Only the English versions of both websites were collected.
  • It is a prototype: no user accounts, no lead capture, no connection to a CRM.

If this went to production

  • Live data from Wasalt's listing API and DarGlobal's project database instead of scraping.
  • Register interest from the chat: hand qualified leads (project, budget, timeline) to the sales team.
  • Arabic sources and a stronger multilingual search model.
  • Feedback and analytics: what people ask, where answers fail, which projects draw interest.
  • More channels: WhatsApp and the companies' own websites.
